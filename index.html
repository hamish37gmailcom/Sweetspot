Sweet Spot Finder

Sweet Spot Finder

Measures how much later one speaker's sound reaches the microphone than the other's, to a fraction of a sample at 48 kHz. Zero means you're on the plane halfway between the speakers.

Test signal

A one-second loop. The left channel plays a rising sweep (1.5–16 kHz, 100 ms) and the right channel plays the same sweep backwards, exactly half a second later. Everything stays above the subwoofer's range.

Plays through this computer's current audio output. Turn BACCH off first, and start with the volume low.

Or download it

Listen with the microphone

The iPhone's microphone is on its bottom edge, beside the charging port. Hold the phone flat at ear height with the bottom edge pointing at the midpoint between the speakers. A tripod phone clamp helps a lot.

Geometry

How the measurement works
  • Each sweep is matched-filtered against its reference, which gives that speaker's impulse response. The rising and falling sweeps have the same spectrum, so neither channel is biased.
  • Sub-sample timing comes from cross-correlating the left and right direct-sound windows (−0.5 to +1.5 ms around the peak), upsampled ×16.
  • The phone and the Mac run on separate clocks, which differ by tens of ppm. Each reading uses three consecutive bursts (L-R-L or R-L-R) and takes half the difference of the two intervals, which cancels the clock difference.
  • The reading includes any delay difference inside the playback chain. With identical speakers and no per-channel delay, zero is the geometric centre plane.
